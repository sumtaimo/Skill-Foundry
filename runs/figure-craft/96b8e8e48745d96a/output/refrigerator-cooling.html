How a refrigerator keeps food cold

A refrigerator moves heat out of the food cabinet. A liquid refrigerant takes heat in at the cold coil, and gives it up at the coil outside the cabinet.

The parts

Food cabinet
The insulated box that holds the food. The refrigerant loop keeps the air inside it cold. The door seals with a rubber gasket.
Evaporator (cold coil)
Refrigerant boils in this coil and takes heat from the air around it. In a real refrigerator it sits inside the cabinet; here it is drawn beside the cabinet so you can see it.
Evaporator fan
Moves air across the cold coil and into the cabinet. Its exact position varies between models; here it sits on top of the coil.
Compressor
The sealed motor that squeezes the refrigerant gas. Squeezing it makes it hot.
Condenser coil
The hot-side coil. The refrigerant gives up its heat here to the room air. In a real refrigerator it is at the back or the base.
Heat moving out
A marker that shows the direction heat travels: out of the cabinet and toward the condenser. It is not a part.

Proportions are approximate. Some parts are drawn beside the cabinet so that each one can be seen; in a real refrigerator the evaporator and its fan sit inside the cabinet, and the condenser sits at the back or the base.
