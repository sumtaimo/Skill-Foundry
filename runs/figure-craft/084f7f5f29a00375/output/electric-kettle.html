How an electric kettle boils water

An electric kettle boils water by sending current through a heating element at the bottom of the jug, which heats the water until it boils.

Kettle body
Holds the water while it is heated.
Where it sits: The round jug that stands on the base. In this view its front-left quarter is cut open so the inside shows.Source: General layout of a common cordless electric kettle; no model was supplied, so this is the usual arrangement.
Lid
Closes the top of the jug. Its knob lifts it off.
Where it sits: Sits on the open top (the mouth) of the jug when closed; here it is lifted clear above the jug, on its hinge.Source: General layout of a common cordless electric kettle; no model was supplied, so this is the usual arrangement.
Handle
Lets you lift the hot jug without touching the wall.
Where it sits: Fixed by two bars to the outside of the wall on the right-hand side, opposite the spout.Source: General layout of a common cordless electric kettle; no model was supplied, so this is the usual arrangement.
Spout
Lets you pour the boiled water out.
Where it sits: Fixed to the outside of the wall near the top, on the side opposite the handle.Source: General layout of a common cordless electric kettle; no model was supplied, so this is the usual arrangement.
Heating elementAssumed
Turns electric current into heat and warms the water from below.
Where it sits: Inside the jug, on its inner floor under the water. Many models hide it in the base of the jug.Source: General layout of a common cordless electric kettle; no model was supplied, so this is the usual arrangement.
Water
The water being boiled. It is drawn as clear glass so the element stays visible.
Where it sits: Inside the jug, filling its lower part up to the waterline on the wall.Source: The waterline is a typical fill level, drawn for the figure and not measured.
Power base
Holds the jug and passes power to it through its contacts.
Where it sits: The separate base under the jug, on four feet. The contacts, the power light and the switch sit on it, and the cord leaves its right-hand side.Source: General layout of a common cordless electric kettle; no model was supplied, so this is the usual arrangement.
Rising bubbleAssumed
Shows heat moving up: a bubble rises from the element through the water.
Where it sits: Starts on the floor of the jug near the element and rises through the water toward the surface.Source: Illustration of boiling. It is not a part and is not to scale.
