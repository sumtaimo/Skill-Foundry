How a refrigerator keeps food cold

A refrigerator keeps food cold by moving heat from inside the cabinet to the coil on its back.

Fresh-food cabinet
The insulated box for everyday food. Its walls keep the room heat out and the cold in.
Freezer
The coldest compartment, above the fresh-food cabinet. The evaporator coil sits at its back.
Door and bins
The door closes on a rubber seal. Bins on its inner side hold bottles and small items.
Glass shelf
Holds food and lets cold air pass round it.
Crisper drawer
A closed drawer for vegetables, kept apart from the cold air that moves past the shelves.
Evaporator coil
The cold coil in the freezer. The refrigerant takes heat out of the air here.
Cold air
Chilled air falls from the vent and moves over the food. This is the one moving part of the story.
Compressor
Pumps the refrigerant round the loop. It sits in the base at the back; turn the fridge to see it.
Condenser coil
The coil on the back. The refrigerant gives up its heat to the room here.
