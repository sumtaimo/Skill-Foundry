How a refrigerator keeps food cold

A refrigerator keeps food cold by pumping heat out of the cabinet: the compressor drives a refrigerant loop that takes heat in at the evaporator and gives it out at the condenser.

The parts

Door and seal
Closes the cold box. The gasket seals the edge so warm room air stays out, and the handle opens it. The dark dot at the top left is the hinge pin.
Evaporator coil
Tubing inside the freezer. Cold liquid refrigerant boils here and takes heat from the air around it.
Evaporator fan
Blows air across the evaporator coil so the cold air spreads into the cabinet. Drawn larger than real.
Compressor
A sealed motor-pump. It squeezes the refrigerant gas, which makes it hot and pushes it round the loop. The dark dot on top marks its electrical terminals.
Condenser coil
Gives the refrigerant's heat to the room. Drawn in the base; on many fridges it sits at the back.
Expansion device
A narrow restriction, often a capillary tube. It drops the pressure so the refrigerant turns cold and liquid before the evaporator. The bold line on top is its tube port. Drawn larger than real.
Cold air
A moving marker for the cold air across the food. It is not a part.
